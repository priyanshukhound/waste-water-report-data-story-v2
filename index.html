The demand

2,800 MLD today, 4,298 MLD by 2035

Freshwater demand follows the people. Three in five litres come from the Kaveri through BWSSB, and the rest is pumped from the ground.

The waste

Most sewage is treated. Little of it comes back.

2,250MLD of sewage

Three out of four litres get treated. Only about 15% of that treated water is used again in the city, so most of it leaves the system.

The 17% treated on site is where apartments matter. Those plants stand next to the people who produce the sewage, which makes their water the easiest to reuse nearby.

The sources

Two kinds of plants treat the city's sewage

Central plants treat most of it, about 1,350 MLD or 60% of the 2,250 MLD of sewage. On-site plants, built inside apartment complexes, treat about 383 MLD or 17%.

The Bengaluru Water Supply and Sewerage Board runs 34 centralised plants with 1,523 MLD of capacity, working at about 88% and treating 1,348 MLD. Only four give tertiary treatment, and about half the treated water goes to lakes outside the city. By 2029, 45 more plants are planned, adding 1,613 MLD.

On-site plants

Only 194 of 330 apartments had a working plant

Of the 330 apartments in the Green Star Challenge, only 194 (58%) had an operational sewage treatment plant. The study also used BWSSB and Karnataka State Pollution Control Board data to understand the designed and operational capacity of on-site plants.

Three-fourths of apartments chose Sequencing Batch Reactors, which are compact and flexible. Activated Sludge is simpler at larger scale; Membrane Bioreactors give the best water at higher cost. Disinfection uses chlorine, UV or ozone.

Reuse across the 194 plants

Purposes: flushing, landscaping, common area cleaning and car wash.

Residential sinks

Apartments and gated communities are the highest potential customers of recycled water, due to their proximity to the source and the nature of domestic use cases

212MLD residential reuse demand, 2025

The 2024 Treated Water Reuse Policy lets a complex sell up to 50% of its treated output, at a lower price than tanker water.

Of 1,004 projects, 549 are completed or ongoing. The 455 blinking new launches have plants yet to be built.

Industrial sinks

Industry wants volume and quality

155MLD industrial reuse demand, 2025

Industry needs tertiary-treated water. About 32 data centres hold 272 MW, and that capacity is expected to double in ten years.

Commercial sinks

Cooling and flushing take 134 MLD

Over 80 IT parks use 57.8 MLD for cooling and 42.5 MLD for flushing. Hotels need 14.5 and 10 MLD, and malls and high streets 6.2 and 2.9 MLD. Demand is expected to rise to 249 MLD in ten years.

Construction sinks

Building sites sit close to the sources

655of 769 sites within 1 km of a project

New apartments need about 49 MLD for curing and earthworks, and commercial building about 26 MLD. Construction peaks in the dry season, when freshwater is scarcest.

Construction sink

Dust suppression is the largest unclaimed use

Roads cover about 24 million m² and need 24 MLD for dust suppression in the dry season. The network is 3,767 km. New construction sites are shown beneath the roads, since building is a big part of dust suppression. Over the next ten years, new built-up area and roads could need 200 MLD for curing and dust control.

Parks and lakes

Parks drink from borewells and tankers

1,161of 2,614 parks within 500 m of a project

Public and private green space needs 35.7 MLD, now met mostly by borewells and, in dry months, tankers. Lakes can take water too: they recharge groundwater at about 3 mm a day, around 35 MLD, across 23.9 million m² at monsoon peak.

Overall potential

644 MLD of demand could take recycled water

On-site plants running at full capacity could offset 20% of freshwater demand. Recycled-water demand more than doubles by 2035, and every sink on the previous pages adds to it.
